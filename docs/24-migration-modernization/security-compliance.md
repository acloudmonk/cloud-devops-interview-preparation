# Security, Compliance, and Governance

[← Validation and operations](validation-operations.md) · [Next: Modernization and value →](modernization-value.md)

## Preserve control objectives through transition

Map identities, data flows, trust boundaries, threats, regulatory obligations, and
current controls to the target and coexistence states. A control may change mechanism
without losing its objective. Record evidence owner, collection method, retention,
exceptions, and approval.

## Secure identities and administration

Federate workforce access, separate human and workload identities, apply least
privilege, protect break-glass paths, rotate credentials, eliminate embedded keys,
and log privileged actions. Include migration tooling, vendors, temporary operators,
pipelines, data-transfer agents, and legacy systems in the identity model.

## Protect data in motion and at rest

Classify data, minimize copies, encrypt transport/storage, control keys, tokenize or
mask where appropriate, preserve audit, validate residency, and define temporary-copy
retention and destruction. Replication, snapshots, logs, support bundles, and test data
can cross boundaries unnoticed.

## Govern exceptions and supply chain

Time-limited exceptions need accountable risk acceptance, compensating control,
expiry, and tracked removal. Assess migration appliances, SaaS tools, agents, images,
libraries, partners, and managed services for provenance, access, data handling,
vulnerability, support, and exit.

## Maintain assurance during change

Automate policy and evidence where possible, but combine it with threat modeling,
architecture review, testing, data reconciliation, access review, recovery evidence,
and independent challenge for high-risk waves. Security approval is not permanent when
scope, architecture, data, or threat assumptions change.
