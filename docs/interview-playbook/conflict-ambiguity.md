# Ambiguity, Conflict, and Difficult Conversations

[← Workshop facilitation](workshop-facilitation.md) · [Next: Options and value →](options-business-case.md)

Conflict often reveals different outcomes, risk tolerances, incentives, time horizons,
or evidence—not difficult personalities.

## Resolve the work, not the person

1. State the shared decision and observed disagreement neutrally.
2. Let each party describe outcome, evidence, fear, and non-negotiable boundary.
3. Separate facts from interpretations and positions from interests.
4. Identify which assumption or criterion causes the options to diverge.
5. Seek data, a reversible experiment, phased decision, or decision-owner ruling.
6. Record dissent, residual risk, and escalation; do not claim consensus.

Use phrases such as: “What evidence would change this view?”, “Which risk are we
choosing?”, and “Is this a legal constraint, policy, preference, or current limitation?”

## When to stop

Pause or escalate when required decision authority is absent, material evidence is
withheld, safety/compliance is at risk, the requested conclusion is predetermined, or
the engagement creates an undisclosed conflict of interest. Document the blocker and
the minimum condition for responsible continuation.
