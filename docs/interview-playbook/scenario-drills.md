# Advanced Facilitation Drills

[← Stakeholder scenarios](scenarios.md) · [Next: Rapid fire →](rapid-fire.md)

## 1. Hidden sponsor conflict

The CIO rewards speed while the CRO rewards zero findings, and neither acknowledges
the trade-off. Interview them separately, state the competing outcomes neutrally,
quantify consequences, create tiered controls, and require the named decision owner to
accept residual risk. Do not let architecture conceal a governance decision.

## 2. Predetermined assessment

You are asked to “independently” confirm a selected product. Clarify the engagement and
evidence threshold in writing. Offer a fitness/risk assessment instead of fake selection,
disclose constraints, and withdraw or escalate if an honest conclusion is prohibited.

## 3. Workshop becomes a blame session

Interrupt personal attribution, restate the decision and working agreement, move claims
to observable timeline/evidence, separate incident learning from performance management,
and pause if safety cannot be restored. Capture systemic conditions and owners.

## 4. Critical user group was excluded

Mark the confidence gap and postpone irreversible approval. Gather representative user
evidence through accessible channels, assess impact on criteria and design, and document
who was previously omitted and how future governance prevents recurrence.

## 5. Executive demands a single cost number

Provide a range with assumptions, demand scenarios, confidence, exclusions, and
sensitivity. Explain which variables dominate. Offer a date and evidence plan to narrow
the range rather than communicating false precision.

## 6. Vendor controls all evidence

Request customer-owned telemetry, references, contractual proof, security material, and
a representative pilot. Mark vendor assertions separately, test exit and failure, and
surface procurement incentives or conflicts before recommendation.

## 7. Decision repeatedly reopens

Show the decision log, original assumptions, and exit triggers. Ask what new evidence
exists. If none, enforce governance; if material evidence changed, reopen only affected
criteria with owner and deadline rather than restarting everything.

## 8. Remote workshop has silent disagreement

Use anonymous pre-input, polls only for sensing, structured rounds, breakout groups,
and explicit dissent capture. Follow up across time zones and cultures. Do not interpret
cameras, silence, or chat absence as agreement.

## 9. Transformation overload

Map dependencies, scarce roles, concurrent change, and operational capacity. Sequence
thin outcomes, stop lower-value work, protect service ownership, and give leaders a
portfolio trade-off rather than another impossible roadmap.

## 10. Recommendation threatens jobs

Treat the human impact as material. Involve leadership and people functions, avoid
promises outside your authority, protect confidentiality, include reskilling/transition
options, and evaluate adoption, ethics, continuity, and knowledge-transfer risk.
