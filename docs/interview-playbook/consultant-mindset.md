# Consultant Mindset and Engagement Lifecycle

[← Playbook overview](index.md) · [Next: Discovery framework →](discovery-framework.md)

A consultant improves decision quality and adoption. The deliverable is not the largest
diagram; it is a defensible decision, shared understanding, feasible change, named
ownership, and evidence that value and risk can be measured.

## Engagement lifecycle

1. **Contract:** agree scope, decision, sponsor, stakeholders, cadence, access, and ethics.
2. **Discover:** understand outcomes, current state, constraints, incentives, and evidence.
3. **Frame:** write decision statement, criteria, assumptions, unknowns, and boundaries.
4. **Explore:** develop credible options, including improve, buy, build, migrate, or defer.
5. **Decide:** compare value, risk, cost, time, reversibility, and organizational readiness.
6. **Mobilize:** define roadmap, owners, dependencies, controls, measures, and next experiment.
7. **Validate:** confirm adoption, realized outcomes, residual risk, and changed assumptions.

## Professional stance

Be curious without being passive, independent without ignoring context, and candid
without humiliating participants. Distinguish evidence from opinion. State uncertainty,
protect confidential information, expose conflicts of interest, and never manufacture
agreement to make a workshop appear successful.

## Three lenses

- **Business:** customer, value, urgency, economics, regulation, strategic fit.
- **Technology:** quality attributes, data, dependencies, failure, security, operations.
- **Change:** skills, incentives, ownership, process, capacity, sequencing, adoption.

A technically elegant recommendation that the organization cannot operate or adopt is
not an effective architecture recommendation.
