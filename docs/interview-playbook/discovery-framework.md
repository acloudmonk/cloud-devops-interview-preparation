# Discovery Framework

[← Consultant mindset](consultant-mindset.md) · [Next: Stakeholders →](stakeholder-mapping.md)

## Frame before questioning

Write: “By **date**, **decision owner** must decide **decision** so that **outcome**,
measured by **metrics**, can be achieved within **constraints**.” Confirm what is out of
scope and what happens if no decision is made.

## Discovery domains

| Domain | Explore | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome | users, value, urgency, success, harm | strategy, KPIs, customer research |
| Current state | flows, ownership, pain, strengths, debt | diagrams, inventory, incidents |
| Demand | volume, peaks, growth, geography | telemetry, forecasts, campaigns |
| Quality | latency, availability, RTO/RPO, integrity | SLOs, tests, incident history |
| Data/security | classification, residency, retention, threats | register, policy, audits |
| Delivery/operations | release, on-call, support, recovery | pipelines, runbooks, restore tests |
| Economics | budget, unit cost, contracts, people | billing, forecasts, staffing |
| Change | skills, incentives, dependencies, readiness | interviews, team topology, roadmap |

## Question ladder

Start open (“Walk me through an order”), probe (“Where does it fail?”), quantify
(“How often and with what impact?”), test contradiction (“Finance reports a different
cost; what explains it?”), and confirm (“I heard X; is that accurate?”).

Maintain a live register: fact, source, confidence, assumption, hypothesis, unknown,
decision, owner, due date. Do not turn an interview into an interrogation or ask
questions whose answers will not influence a decision.
