# Stakeholder Scenarios and Model Direction

[← Templates](templates.md) · [Next: Advanced drills →](scenario-drills.md)

## 1. The sponsor asks for a multicloud strategy

**Model direction:** Clarify the risk or outcome, decision, deadline, workload scope,
and success measures. Test sovereignty, resilience, commercial, acquisition, and
innovation claims. Compare simpler options and expose duplicated operations and data.

## 2. “The system must never go down”

**Model direction:** Ask which journeys and failure domains matter, quantify impact and
budget, distinguish availability from recovery, define SLI/SLO/RTO/RPO, and present
tiered options with cost and residual risk.

## 3. Finance and engineering report different cloud costs

**Model direction:** Align scope, period, allocation, shared cost, credits, commitments,
and business denominator. Preserve both sources, reconcile differences, assign data
ownership, and avoid choosing the number that supports a preferred option.

## 4. Security joins at final approval and rejects the design

**Model direction:** Pause the approval, identify threat and policy evidence, classify
must-fix versus residual risk, add the accountable risk owner, and revise engagement
governance so security participates during discovery and options.

## 5. The executive already selected a vendor

**Model direction:** Clarify whether the decision is selection or implementation and
ask what evidence or constraint could change it. Document the mandate, still validate
fitness and risks, and refuse to fabricate an independent comparison.

## 6. Operators disagree with the platform team

**Model direction:** Map outcomes, workload evidence, toil, support boundaries, skills,
and incentives. Walk incidents and operational journeys, define acceptance measures,
and run a reversible pilot rather than settling by organizational rank.

## 7. A stakeholder dominates the workshop

**Model direction:** Restate working agreements, use silent input and round-robin
participation, redirect to criteria/evidence, invite affected voices, and follow up
privately if behavior continues. Preserve psychological safety without avoiding candor.

## 8. Nobody will own the final decision

**Model direction:** Stop pretending the meeting can decide. Escalate with a concise
decision brief, options, deadline, consequence of delay, and proposed owner. Assign
actions only to people who accept them.

## 9. The migration deadline cannot move

**Model direction:** Treat time as a constraint, not permission to ignore risk. Reduce
scope, stage waves, identify invariants, increase evidence early, define rollback and
contingency, and show which risk the decision owner explicitly accepts.

## 10. Data is unavailable for sizing

**Model direction:** Use ranges, proxies, sampling, and short instrumentation; state
confidence and sensitivity. Design a reversible capacity step with guardrails rather
than turning an unsupported point estimate into fact.

## 11. Two regulations appear contradictory

**Model direction:** Do not interpret law as the architect. Capture data/jurisdiction/
process, obtain qualified legal and compliance decisions, explore architectures after
constraints are clarified, and record authority, rationale, and residual risk.

## 12. The team requests a product comparison only

**Model direction:** Reframe around outcomes and capability requirements, then compare
viable products on weighted criteria, failure, operations, cost, adoption, and exit.
Provide the requested artifact without endorsing feature-count selection.

## 13. A pilot succeeded but adoption stalled

**Model direction:** Compare pilot conditions with real workflows, incentives, support,
skills, migration effort, and product usability. Interview non-adopters, measure time
to value, fix the operating model, and change or stop the product if evidence demands.

## 14. A major assumption changes after approval

**Model direction:** Use the decision record and exit trigger, quantify affected
criteria, reconvene the decision owner and impacted stakeholders, compare adapt/stop/
continue, and preserve the audit trail rather than quietly redesigning.

## 15. Present a recommendation to the board

**Model direction:** Lead with decision and outcome, explain options and why now, give
investment ranges, major risk and mitigation, staged value, confidence, owner, and
specific ask. Keep technical evidence available for questions.
