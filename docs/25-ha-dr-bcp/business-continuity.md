# Business Continuity and Crisis Management

[← Module overview](index.md) · [Multi-Region failover](multi-region-failover.md)

Technology recovery is one workstream within business continuity. A system can be
available while the business cannot operate because people, facilities, suppliers,
communications, decision rights, or manual procedures are unavailable.

## Continuity plan components

- critical products, minimum acceptable service, and prioritization rules;
- executive sponsor, crisis lead, alternates, decision thresholds, and authority;
- emergency notification and out-of-band communication channels;
- workforce availability, succession, remote-work, facility, and safety arrangements;
- supplier dependencies, contractual recovery commitments, and alternatives;
- manual workarounds with security, privacy, capacity, and reconciliation controls;
- customer, regulator, partner, employee, and media communication ownership;
- technology recovery plans, cyber response, and return-to-normal criteria;
- exercise schedule, evidence, lessons, and corrective-action governance.

## Command layers

Keep responsibilities connected but distinct:

- **Incident command** stabilizes the technical service.
- **Disaster-recovery leadership** coordinates technology restoration and validation.
- **Crisis management** makes enterprise risk, safety, priority, and communication decisions.
- **Business continuity teams** operate alternate processes and manage business backlog.

One person should not be expected to lead all layers during a major event. Define
liaisons, update cadence, shared facts, and escalation triggers.

## Manual workaround test

A workaround is credible only if volume, staffing, access, fraud/security checks,
data capture, privacy, approvals, and later reconciliation are understood. “Use a
spreadsheet” may introduce data leakage, duplicate transactions, and an impossible
backlog. State the maximum duration and throughput of the alternate process.

## Third-party continuity

Map concentration risk beyond the named vendor: common cloud Regions, identity,
DNS, telecommunications, software supply chains, payment networks, and support
channels. Review evidence and contracts, but also design timeouts, alternate
providers or processes, data export, exit plans, and tested contact paths.

## Communication principle

Communicate confirmed impact, actions, decisions needed, next update time, and
uncertainty. Do not promise a recovery time unsupported by evidence. Record major
decisions and assumptions for handover, regulatory reporting, and learning.
