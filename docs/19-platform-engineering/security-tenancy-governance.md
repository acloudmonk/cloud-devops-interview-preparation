# Security, Tenancy, and Governance

[← Delivery and supply chain](delivery-supply-chain.md) · [Next: Reliability and platform operations →](reliability-operations.md)

## Guardrails with responsibility

Prevent a small set of unacceptable states, detect and remediate broader posture,
and make exceptions explicit, time-bound, owned, and reviewed. Policy-as-code makes
decisions consistent but does not define correct policy by itself.

## Tenancy and isolation

Choose account/project, cluster, namespace, network, data, key, runner, and control-
plane boundaries from threat, compliance, noisy-neighbor, failure, and cost needs.
Shared infrastructure can be efficient; it requires tenant identity everywhere,
quotas, authorization, cache/log isolation, and tested boundary controls.

## Privilege model

- federate people and require elevated just-in-time access;
- issue short-lived workload and controller identities;
- scope reconcilers to cells or environments rather than the organization;
- isolate build from deploy and deploy from runtime;
- protect secrets and signing keys outside user-supplied execution;
- audit intent, policy, approval, action, and result.

## Governance evidence

Build evidence into the path: ownership, classification, threat model, approvals,
provenance, scan results, policy decisions, deployment, SLO, backup, and exception.
Evidence must be accurate and retrievable, not a compliance checkbox generated by
a template and never revalidated.

## Escape hatches and shadow platforms

Provide a documented path for unsupported needs with risk owner, minimum controls,
support boundary, expiry, and re-entry plan. Investigate bypass behavior as product
feedback. Blocking every variation can increase risk by moving work outside visibility.
