# Facilitation and Stakeholder Communication

[← Module overview](index.md) · [Trade-offs and decisions](tradeoffs-decisions.md)

Review quality depends on whether participants can surface uncertainty, incidents,
workarounds, and constraints without expecting blame. Psychological safety does not
mean avoiding challenge; it means challenging the system and claim with respect and evidence.

## Session design

- Share purpose, scope, non-audit position, participants, agenda, and evidence expectations.
- Use a neutral facilitator and a separate note taker for complex sessions.
- Begin with business journeys and architecture narrative before framework questions.
- Time-box topics but create a parking lot for specialist follow-up.
- Ask owners to demonstrate evidence; do not interrogate individuals for trivia.
- Summarize decisions, disagreements, assumptions, and actions before closing.

Run multiple focused sessions for large workloads. Long questionnaire marathons reduce
attention and encourage “yes” answers without evidence.

## Constructive challenge

Replace “Why didn’t you follow best practice?” with “Which requirement led to this
choice, what failure did you plan for, and what evidence shows it is safe?” Distinguish
constraints from preferences and current state from target state. Invite operations,
security, product, and finance to correct architecture-only assumptions.

When evidence conflicts, record both sources, time, environment, and owner; assign
validation rather than forcing agreement in the room. When serious active risk appears,
follow incident or security escalation procedures instead of waiting for the report.

## Difficult stakeholder patterns

- **Defensive owner:** reaffirm learning purpose; focus on system history and support needed.
- **Executive solutioning:** restate outcome and evidence before accepting a preferred product.
- **Dominant specialist:** round-robin quieter owners and separate specialist deep dives.
- **Absent decision maker:** mark recommendations pending rather than inventing acceptance.
- **Framework lawyer:** return to business risk and current official guidance.
- **Sales pressure:** disclose interests and preserve provider-neutral options.

## Reporting layers

Engineers need evidence and implementation context. Product owners need customer and
roadmap impact. Risk/compliance need control and acceptance traceability. Executives
need top risks, choices, investment, dependencies, and residual exposure. Maintain one
source of findings while tailoring summaries without changing facts.

## Independence and ethics

Disclose commercial incentives and conflicts. Do not manufacture severity, promise
certification, hide uncertainty, or use sensitive review data outside scope. A reviewer
should be willing to recommend no change when evidence shows the design is appropriate.
