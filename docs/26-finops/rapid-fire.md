# Rapid-Fire Revision

[← Module overview](index.md) · [Active-recall flashcards](flashcards.md)

Answer each in one or two sentences. Expand only when challenged.

1. **What is FinOps?** A collaborative operating framework that maximizes technology business value through timely data and shared financial accountability.
2. **Is FinOps only cost reduction?** No; it balances value, cost, quality, speed, and risk.
3. **Who owns FinOps?** Engineering, finance, product, procurement, leadership, and practitioners own different decisions collaboratively.
4. **What are Inform, Optimize, and Operate?** Iterative FinOps phases for visibility/accountability, improvement, and continuous governance.
5. **What is a FinOps scope?** A defined technology-spend boundary aligned to business constructs and outcomes.
6. **Why start with outcome?** A lower bill can destroy more value than it saves if customer, reliability, or delivery impact is ignored.
7. **What is allocation?** Mapping cost and usage to accountable business constructs using structural, metadata, and measured rules.
8. **Why are tags insufficient?** Some costs are untaggable, shared, delayed, or affected by metadata changes.
9. **What is unallocated cost?** Spend without a defensible owner or allocation rule; expose it and drive remediation.
10. **What is showback?** Visibility of attributed cost without transferring financial charge.
11. **What is chargeback?** Formal assignment of cost to an accountable financial entity.
12. **When should chargeback start?** After data, rules, shared costs, adjustments, and disputes are trusted.
13. **What is unit economics?** Cost related to a meaningful business or workload unit and paired with outcome quality.
14. **What makes a good unit?** It correlates with demand/value, is consistently measurable, and enables action without harmful incentives.
15. **Why pair cost per unit with quality?** A cheaper failed, slow, or insecure transaction is not better value.
16. **What is unblended cost?** A line-item usage cost view before certain sharing or portfolio effects.
17. **What is amortized cost?** A view spreading upfront commitment fees over their benefit period.
18. **What is net cost?** A cost view incorporating applicable discounts, credits, or adjustments where provided.
19. **Why can invoice and dashboard differ?** Scope, timing, currency, tax, credits, support, marketplace, and cost basis may differ.
20. **What is CUR 2.0?** AWS detailed cost and usage data delivered through Data Exports with a configurable schema.
21. **What is FOCUS?** An open specification for normalizing cost and usage data across technology vendors.
22. **Does FOCUS erase provider differences?** No; service, price, discount, identity, and optimization semantics remain provider-specific.
23. **What is a driver-based forecast?** A projection derived from business demand, architecture, price, commitments, and known events.
24. **Why forecast a range?** Demand, delivery, pricing, and optimization are uncertain; one number hides risk.
25. **Is a budget a hard limit?** Not necessarily; it is an agreed plan and control with defined responses.
26. **What is variance analysis?** Explaining difference through price, quantity, mix, timing, allocation, and adjustments.
27. **What is anomaly management?** Detecting, assessing, containing, explaining, and preventing material unexpected cost change.
28. **Can cost anomaly detection prevent spend?** No; provider billing data is delayed and detection requires an owned response.
29. **Usage versus rate optimization?** Change consumption and architecture versus change price paid for eligible consumption.
30. **Why optimize usage before commitments?** Otherwise the organization may commit to waste or soon-to-migrate demand.
31. **What is rightsizing?** Matching resource configuration to workload demand and operational guardrails.
32. **Why can rightsizing be unsafe?** Average utilization ignores peaks, failures, scaling delay, quotas, and resilience headroom.
33. **What is realized savings?** Verified cost reduction after implementation, demand change, fees, and quality impact.
34. **What is estimated savings?** A modeled opportunity that has not yet been implemented or verified.
35. **What is commitment coverage?** The share of eligible usage receiving commitment benefit.
36. **What is commitment utilization?** The share of purchased commitment consumed.
37. **Can coverage and utilization conflict?** Yes; maximizing one may reduce the other or increase inflexibility.
38. **Do reservations always reserve capacity?** No; discount and capacity behavior depends on the specific provider construct.
39. **What is Spot suitable for?** Interruptible, fault-tolerant workloads with checkpointing, diversification, and fallback.
40. **How should Kubernetes cost be viewed?** Cloud infrastructure plus shared platform, workload allocation, idle capacity, and business attribution.
41. **Requests or usage for Kubernetes allocation?** Show both; requests influence capacity while usage shows consumption.
42. **How should idle cluster cost be treated?** Expose it before allocating via a transparent policy; distinguish required headroom from waste.
43. **How do you value a platform?** Pair total/marginal cost with adoption, lead time, reliability, security, support, and duplication removed.
44. **What is a safe budget action?** Workload-scoped, approved, tested, reversible, auditable, and protected by criticality/SLO controls.
45. **What should a FinOps policy include?** Scope, rule, owner, enforcement, evidence, exception, expiry, and review trigger.
46. **What is a good FinOps cadence?** A frequency matched to materiality and data latency with decisions, owners, and outcome follow-up.
47. **How do sustainability and cost relate?** Efficiency may improve both, but carbon and currency require separate data and explicit trade-offs.
48. **What belongs in multi-cloud TCO?** Egress, duplicated platforms, skills, support, commitments, security, operations, migration, and risk.
49. **What measures FinOps success?** Better business-value decisions and realized outcomes, not dashboard count or maturity score alone.
50. **Best closing interview point?** State outcome, driver, decision, owner, guardrails, realized measure, and residual risk.
