# Advanced Cost and Value Drills

[← Module overview](index.md) · [Scenario questions](scenarios.md)

Use five minutes per drill. Establish facts, business impact, cost basis, owner,
safe containment, longer-term decision, evidence, and communication.

## Drill 1 — NAT and cross-zone charges spike

A release changes traffic paths and network cost rises sixfold. Trace deployment,
flow, service and usage-type data; contain without bypassing security; decide
topology changes; and verify latency, resilience, and realized savings.

## Drill 2 — Serverless runaway invocation loop

Function invocations and downstream requests accelerate while customer traffic is
flat. Address kill switches, quotas, idempotency, retry limits, security, financial
exposure, customer impact, and preventive testing.

## Drill 3 — Forecast misses after acquisition

New accounts, contracts, currencies, and duplicate platforms enter mid-quarter.
Rebaseline scope and drivers, separate integration costs, reconcile commitments,
define ownership, and communicate a forecast range rather than false precision.

## Drill 4 — Reserved commitment expires next week

The owner left and no renewal decision exists. Inventory eligible demand and
migrations, model short-term on-demand exposure versus commitment risk, assign
authority, make a bounded decision, and create lifecycle alerts and succession.

## Drill 5 — Storage lifecycle breaks compliance retrieval

An optimization moves records to a tier that cannot meet retrieval obligations.
Stop further transition, restore access, quantify impact, involve compliance, and
redesign lifecycle policy using classification, access evidence, and recovery tests.

## Drill 6 — Shared platform has no adoption metric

Platform cost rises while teams claim it improves delivery. Define marginal and
fully loaded cost, adoption, duplicated tooling removed, lead time, reliability,
security coverage, support demand, and product owner decisions.

## Drill 7 — AI inference unit cost is unstable

Model, hardware, token mix, cache rate, latency, and customer tiers change rapidly.
Design cost per useful outcome, forecast scenarios, quota/anomaly controls, routing,
model choice, capacity/commitment strategy, and quality guardrails.

## Drill 8 — Credits hide poor unit economics

A product looks profitable until promotional credits expire. Report gross and net
views, expiry, underlying usage/rate drivers, future steady-state unit cost, owner,
and a product or architecture decision before the cliff.

## Drill 9 — Spot interruption creates an expensive fallback

Spot saves compute cost but repeated interruption moves work to a costly synchronous
path. Evaluate total completed-work cost, queue and checkpoint design, reliability,
deadline, fallback rate, and whether a different capacity portfolio is better.

## Drill 10 — Cost dashboard exposes confidential rates

Broad dashboard access reveals negotiated pricing and sensitive product/customer
allocation. Restrict views, preserve necessary team visibility, audit access,
classify fields, create persona-specific datasets, and update governance.

## Self-review prompts

- Did I identify value and customer impact, not only the bill?
- Did I state scope, time, cost basis, allocation, and data latency?
- Did I separate usage, rate, demand, price, mix, and one-time changes?
- Did I protect reliability, security, compliance, and delivery guardrails?
- Did I name the persona with authority and the evidence they need?
- Did I measure realized outcome and prevent recurrence?
