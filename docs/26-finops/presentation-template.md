# Ten-Minute FinOps Review

[← Module overview](index.md) · [Design exercise](design-exercise.md)

Use this structure to present FinOps to an executive or architecture panel. Lead
with business value and decisions, not a tour of cost dashboards.

## Minute 0–1 — Outcome and scope

State the product, technology categories/providers, billing boundary, period,
business objective, materiality, and why a decision is needed now.

## Minute 1–2 — Data and confidence

Define cost basis, currency, timing, invoice reconciliation, allocation coverage,
quality gaps, adjustments, and which figures are estimates.

## Minute 2–3 — Value and unit economics

Show demand, revenue or outcome, total cost, cost per meaningful unit, quality/SLO
guardrails, trend, and whether growth is efficient.

## Minute 3–4 — Drivers and variance

Decompose price, quantity, mix, architecture, traffic, data, deployment, commitment,
credit, and one-time changes. Distinguish expected growth from avoidable waste.

## Minute 4–5 — Allocation and accountability

Explain ownership hierarchy, shared-cost rules, unallocated amount, showback or
chargeback, dispute path, and accountable product/engineering decisions.

## Minute 5–6 — Optimization portfolio

Present workload, architecture, rate, licensing, platform, and sustainability
actions by value, risk, effort, owner, and delivery date. Protect explicit guardrails.

## Minute 6–7 — Planning and commercial position

Cover driver-based forecast, range and assumptions, budget variance, commitments,
coverage/utilization, expiries, flexibility, and downside scenario.

## Minute 7–8 — Operating model

Describe personas, cadences, policies, enablement, anomaly response, exceptions,
automation boundaries, and how decisions reach engineering workflows.

## Minute 8–9 — Evidence and risk

Separate estimated, implemented, and realized value. Show unit-economic change,
forecast accuracy, anomaly response, quality impact, implementation cost, and
residual risks.

## Minute 9–10 — Recommendation

Summarize the largest value opportunity, immediate action, architectural/commercial
decision, investment, owner, time horizon, trade-off, and executive approval needed.

## Closing sentence pattern

“For **[scope]**, **[driver]** changed **[unit/value measure]**. I recommend
**[action]**, owned by **[persona]**, with **[guardrails]**; we will confirm
**[realized outcome]** by **[date]**, while accepting **[residual risk]**.”
